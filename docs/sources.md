# Financial Information Sources

MoneyMentorr's sources are mostly based in the US and global information is limited as of now.

## Primary Organizations

| Organization                          | Website                                                              | Main Topics                                        |
| ------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------- |
| Consumer Financial Protection Bureau  | [https://www.consumerfinance.gov/](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/) | Consumer protection, credit, banking, debt         |
| Federal Student Aid                   | [https://studentaid.gov/](https://studentaid.gov/)                   | FAFSA, financial aid, student loans                |
| Internal Revenue Service              | [https://www.irs.gov/](https://www.irs.gov/)                         | Taxes, retirement accounts, education tax benefits |
| Federal Deposit Insurance Corporation | [https://www.fdic.gov/](https://www.fdic.gov/)                       | Banking, deposit insurance, consumer education     |
| Investor.gov (SEC)                    | [https://www.investor.gov/](https://www.investor.gov/)               | Investing, diversification, investment risks       |

## How Sources Are Used

MoneyMentor contains a evidence library in `src/data/evidence.js` and a trusted-source library in `src/data/sources.js`.

For selected financial topics, the application provides stored evidence summaries to the Groq  AI tutor.

## Citation Limitations

An official website link does not necessarily verify every statement in an AI-generated answer.

MoneyMentor does not currently perform comprehensive live website retrieval and claim-by-claim verification.

Users should consult official sources for current eligibility requirements, financial limits, deadlines, tax rules, and other information that may change.

## Content and Copyright

Source links and independently written factual summaries are used for educational reference. Public accessibility of a source does not automatically mean its text can be copied, modified, or redistributed without restriction.

The application should avoid reproducing substantial copyrighted passages and should respect the applicable terms and licenses of source materials.
